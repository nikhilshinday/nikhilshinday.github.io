Joy-maxxing is the point.

Nikhil Shinday · November 13, 2026

Yesterday I found myself in a rut. I called a friend and he sent me this audio. He asked me to listen to it at least three times before I called him back. My conclusion after listening to it and talking to him for a while was that I needed to re-cultivate a spiritual practice.

I have the desire to exercise deeply imprinted in my body. I wasn’t always like that: I was obese in my adolescent years and lost weight in young adulthood, but struggled to maintain consistency because I was motivated by The Wrong Things - self-hate, escapism, denialism. During the pandemic, I found a practice that made it stick: I enjoyment-maxxed. “Just keep going” was the mantra that I found myself reciting, and I did it for long enough that my spinal cord has taken over. Most importantly, I did whatever I could to make going to the gym as enjoyable as possible, so that I’d want to do it. The joy has become the point, but that’s also the only way that it could’ve worked.

I’ve had spiritual practices at multiple points in my life. Some of them have been spiritually materialistic. Others have been like the bursty motivation of making a New Year’s Resolution to go to the gym: “I’m going to meditate an hour a day” without knowing why, “I’m going to microdose once a week and free up Sunday afternoons for introspection” without knowing what introspection would give me. But those have the same problems as The Wrong Motivations I described for the gym - they won’t create the conditions under which I can feel joy.

I’m lucky to have joy as an intrinsic experience: there’s a low hum in my body that feels tuned to something enormous and cosmic. I can feel it most of the time if I remember to, but it fades into the background for most of my waking day.

Today I found a "why" for spiritual practice: to amplify that joy into every fiber of my being, into every thought and action that I make, into every moment of perception in my conscious experience.

I’m going to have to pick practices that facilitate that, but at least I have a north star for why I’ll choose one over another: joy-maxxing is the point.
